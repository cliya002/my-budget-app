    📚 Score Education
    Payment History · 35% — what is it?

    The single biggest factor. Records every on-time and late payment across all your accounts. Even one 30-day late can drop your score 60-100 points.

    How to improve: Set up autopay for at least the minimum on every card, never miss a due date, and dispute incorrect late marks.

    Credit Utilization · 30% — what is it?

    Your card balances divided by your total credit limit. Reported at statement close, not due date. Aim for under 10% for top scores.

    How to improve: Pay balances down before the statement closes, request limit increases, or spread spending across multiple cards.

    Length of Credit History · 15% — what is it?

    Average age of all your accounts plus the age of your oldest account. Older = better. Closing your oldest card can hurt this.

    How to improve: Don't close old cards even if unused. Use them once a year to keep them active.

    Credit Mix · 10% — what is it?

    Variety of credit types: credit cards, auto loans, mortgages, student loans. Lenders like to see you can handle different debt types.

    How to improve: Don't take on debt just for mix. This is the smallest factor — focus on payment history and utilization first.

    New Credit · 10% — what is it?

    Hard inquiries (when you apply for new credit) and recently opened accounts. Each hard inquiry costs ~5 points and lasts 24 months.

    How to improve: Space out applications by 6+ months, only apply when you actually need credit.

    📖 Glossary
    FICO vs VantageScore

    Two competing score models, both 300-850. FICO is used by 90%+ of lenders. VantageScore is what Credit Karma shows. They can differ by 30-50 points. Lenders almost always pull FICO.

    Hard vs Soft Inquiry

    Hard: when you apply for credit (loan, new card). Visible to other lenders for 24 months, costs ~5 points. Soft: checking your own score, pre-qualifications, employer checks. No score impact.

    APR (Annual Percentage Rate)

    The yearly interest rate charged on a balance you carry month-to-month. If you pay in full each month, APR doesn't matter.

    Statement Balance vs Current Balance

    Statement balance: what was owed when the billing cycle closed. This is what gets reported to credit bureaus and counts for utilization. Current balance: what you owe right now (statement + new charges). Pay statement balance to avoid interest.

    Credit Utilization

    Total card balance / total credit limit. Reported at statement close. Top scorers stay under 10%. Counted both per-card and overall.
